MathDraw Layer: —

Hey, welcome to MathDraw ✏️

You draw stuff on a math grid, and every stroke you make gets turned into a real equation that works in Desmos or GeoGebra. That's basically it. Here's what you need to know to get going.

✏️
Just start drawing

Pick the Brush and draw anything. Each stroke gets fitted to a curve automatically. You don't have to do anything special.

ƒ(x)
Get your equations

Open the Equations tab on the right. Every stroke shows up there. Hit the D button next to any equation to copy it for Desmos, or export everything as a GeoGebra file.

🔍
Moving around

Scroll to zoom. Hold Space and drag to pan. On mobile, pinch to zoom and use two fingers to move around. Hit the Default View button up top to snap back to the center.

⌖
Lost your drawing?

Press Ctrl+A to select everything, then click Go to Selection in the top bar. It'll zoom straight to where your art is.

Drawing and tools

Brush B

Your main drawing tool. Adjust the size in the right panel. Keep Auto-fit stroke checked and every stroke becomes an equation. Turn it off if you just want to sketch something without generating equations.

Eraser E

Wipes out the stroke and its equation at the same time. If you erase through the middle of a line it splits into two separate curves, each with its own equation.

Shapes R C O

Rectangle, circle or ellipse, and polygon. Each one gives you a clean equation right away. Check Fill in the tool options to also get a shaded inequality version of the shape.

Fill G

Click inside any closed shape and it fills that area with a solid Desmos inequality. Click somewhere empty and it splits the whole canvas into two halves at that y position, one region above and one below.

Edit Points V

Switch to this after drawing and drag the dots on any stroke to reshape it. The equation in the panel updates as you move things.

Selection tools W L P

Magic Wand, Lasso, and Polygonal Lasso. Use these to select an area then move, delete, or fit equations to it. While placing points with the Polygonal Lasso or Polygon tool, Ctrl+Z removes the last point you placed, Esc cancels the whole thing, and Enter or double-click finishes it.

Equations and exporting

The Equations panel

Open the Equations tab at the bottom of the right panel. Every shape you draw shows up here. Each equation line has two buttons next to it:

  • D copies that equation for Desmos. Paste it straight into desmos.com/calculator.
  • G copies the GeoGebra version of that one equation.

Copying everything for Desmos

Click the Desmos button at the top of the Equations tab to grab all equations at once. Then go to Desmos, click any empty expression box, and paste. All your equations load in one shot.

Exporting to GeoGebra

Click GeoGebra to download a .ggb file. Open it in GeoGebra Classic on desktop or at geogebra.org/classic. All your equations load straight in, no pasting needed.

GeoGebra needs explicit multiplication between an exponent and a variable, like ^2*t instead of ^2t. The export fixes this for you automatically so you don't need to worry about it.

Fit types

Head to the Fit tab to choose what kinds of equations your freehand strokes get fitted to: lines, quadratics, cubics, or circles. Cubic is the default and works well for most drawings. Unchecking cubic uses more simpler pieces to cover the same stroke.

Eq. Only mode

The Eq. Only button hides the painted preview and shows just the vector outlines. Good for checking what your art will actually look like when pasted into Desmos.

Layers and reference images

Layers

Open the Layers tab. You can add as many drawing layers as you want, toggle each one on or off, drag them to reorder, merge two together, or duplicate one. Each layer has its own equations in the panel.

Reference images

Click the Reference button in the top bar and pick any image file. It loads as a special layer pinned to the bottom of the stack. A few things worth knowing:

  • It always sits below your drawing layers so it won't cover your work.
  • It has an opacity slider in the layers panel. Drop it to around 20-30% so you can see your strokes clearly on top.
  • You can't draw on reference layers. They're just there to trace over.
  • They show a REF tag in the layers panel so you can tell them apart.

The whole point is you load a sketch or photo, fade it out, then trace over it with the Brush to turn it into Desmos equations.

Undo and redo

Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y work per layer, up to 30 steps back. One thing that's different: while you're placing points with the Polygon tool or Polygonal Lasso, Ctrl+Z removes the last point you placed rather than undoing the whole layer. Once you commit the shape, Ctrl+Z undoes the whole thing as normal.

Saving and keyboard shortcuts

Auto-save

Your work saves to your browser automatically every couple of seconds. If you close the tab and come back, it'll restore where you left off. Nothing to set up.

Save as .mathart Ctrl+S

Downloads a file with all your layers, equations, and view position saved. Open it back up with the Open button or Ctrl+O. Good if you want to back up your work or move it to another device.

Export PNG Ctrl+E

Saves a flat image of what's on screen right now. Useful for sharing a preview of your drawing.
